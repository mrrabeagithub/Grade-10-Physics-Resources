Rectilinear Propagation

The fundamental rule of optics: In a homogeneous, transparent medium, light always travels in straight lines. This explains shadows and why we cannot see around corners.

Sources of Light

To see anything, light must enter our eyes. Objects are classified by how they provide that light. This section compares objects that generate light versus those that merely reflect it.

Luminous Objects

Primary Sources

  • ✓ Generate and emit their own light.
  • ✓ Examples: The Sun, a lightbulb, a candle flame.

Illuminated Objects

Secondary / Non-luminous Sources

  • ✓ Do not produce their own light.
  • ✓ Visible because they reflect light into our eyes.
  • ✓ Examples: The Moon, a mirror, paper.

Light Emission vs Reflection Profile

Theoretical relative intensity. Luminous objects emit high energy, while illuminated objects only reflect a portion of received ambient light.

Light Rays vs. Light Beams

Because light travels in straight lines, we use geometric lines to study it. A Light Ray is a single line with an arrowhead. A Light Beam is a bundle of rays traveling together.

Optical Systems & Images

An optical system takes light from an object and redirects it to form an image. Click the cards below to explore the properties of different images and systems.

Types of Images

📷

Real Images

(Click to reveal properties)

Real Image Properties

  • Formation: Light rays actually meet at a specific point.
  • Orientation: Always inverted (upside down).
  • Projection: CAN be projected onto a physical screen or sensor.
  • Examples: Movie theater screen, camera sensor.
🫾

Virtual Images

(Click to reveal properties)

Virtual Image Properties

  • Formation: Light rays appear to come from a point, but don't actually meet.
  • Orientation: Always upright.
  • Projection: CANNOT be projected onto a screen.
  • Examples: Reflection in a bathroom mirror.

System Type Function Image Size Output
